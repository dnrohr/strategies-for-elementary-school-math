# Reusable chapter-image lessons

Read these as inspection prompts, not automatic edit instructions. Apply a rule only when the current picture exhibits the relevant condition. Add a new entry only when it is materially distinct from an existing rule, and cite the chapter where it arose.

## Motion and sequence

1. **Terminate connector strokes at the base of filled arrowheads.** Do not let a line continue beneath its head. Inspect the join at final reading size. Origin: CH01.
2. **Map every cue to the state it triggers.** Tap/beat marks precede their spoken values; motion cues sit directly over or beside only the components that change. Origin: CH01.
3. **Make sequential identity traceable.** When objects move or regroup, the same units should retain scale, pattern, color role, and count across stages. Origin: CH01.

## Mathematical meaning

4. **Visualize equivalence as conservation, not unexplained travel.** For commutativity or regrouping, visibly match the same quantities across positions; use direction only when direction is mathematically meaningful. Origin: CH01.
5. **Preserve every term through symbolic transformations.** No addend, residual, remainder, or unit may disappear between displayed lines. Origin: CH01.
6. **Keep unit geometry exact.** Equivalent units retain equal apparent size; number-line intervals and fraction partitions remain proportional; counts are auditable. Origin: CH01.

## Composition and labeling

7. **Reserve clear lanes for labels and trajectories.** Put source labels away from outgoing paths and never make readers look through text to follow motion. Origin: CH01.
8. **Avoid repeated explanation inside the image.** Prefer one dominant mathematical representation and a short supporting label over duplicated headings, sequences, and equation rails. Origin: CH01.
9. **Keep props recognizable but subordinate.** Pens, hands, tools, and environmental cues need enough detail to read immediately without competing with the mathematics. Origin: CH01.

## Hands and organic art

10. **Check laterality as well as digit count.** A sequence must preserve one anatomical left/right pair when that is what the scene requires; five plausible digits alone are not sufficient. Origin: CH01.
11. **Choose the starting gesture before placing movement cues.** Define which fingers are extended/folded, then align cues only with the digits that change state in the next panel. Origin: CH01.

## Editorial framing

12. **Avoid redundant constructed-account boilerplate.** When the book-level disclosure is present and an account begins “A solver might describe the experience this way,” remove repeated `Constructed solver voice — not a participant quotation` lines from the chapter while preserving the disclosure and introductory framing. Origin: CH01.

## Temporal state and notation

13. **Label an embodied snapshot between steps.** When a hand or tool sits between two marks in a longer sequence, distinguish completed from pending marks with shape/outline as well as color and name the moment explicitly (for example, “after tap 4”). Origin: CH02.
14. **Strike only the place-value unit being exchanged.** In a regrouping rewrite, crossing an entire numeral can imply deletion of the value; mark only the digit or unit that changes and state the conserved equivalent form nearby. Origin: CH02.

## Equation relationships

15. **Do not place equality signs between complete related equations.** When fact-family cards each contain a full equation, connect them with labeled relational arrows or captions; an equality sign between cards can create a false chain even when every card is individually true. Origin: CH06.
16. **Keep original partitions traceable after refining to a common unit.** When unlike fraction bars are subdivided into the same finer unit, retain subordinate boundaries for each source denominator; do not impose one grouping rhythm on both bars merely because their fine cells now match. Origin: CH09.
17. **Show compound place-value exchanges as ordered states.** When incoming units change the amount available in the next place, label the exchange from that updated state (for example, `21 ones → 22 tens → 12 hundreds`) rather than attaching independent trade captions to the raw inventory. Origin: CH12.

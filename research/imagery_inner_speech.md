# Imagery and inner speech

Task: `R02`
Evidence review date: 2026-09-06

## Search rationale and scope

This note separates four things that are often blurred together: subjective inner speech or imagery; temporary verbal storage/rehearsal; visuospatial storage; and domain-general executive control. Searches emphasized dual-task mental-arithmetic experiments, strategy- and presentation-sensitive studies, developmental reviews/meta-analyses, and a major review of inner-speech phenomenology and measurement.

Dual-task interference and working-memory correlations can identify processing demands under particular conditions. They cannot, by themselves, establish that a participant consciously heard number words or saw a mental picture. Records in `research/source_log.csv` are `verified` only where the article text was checked.

## Findings

### Arithmetic recruits multiple working-memory resources

- In adult multidigit addition, Logie, Gilhooly, and Wynn found the largest disruption from a concurrent random-generation task and smaller effects from articulatory suppression. Effects were much smaller with visual than auditory problem presentation. The authors interpreted the pattern as executive involvement plus a role for subvocal rehearsal in maintaining accuracy; visuospatial resources may contribute to approximation. [R02-I03, Abstract; screened]
- Fürst and Hitch found that articulatory suppression impaired addition of briefly presented three-digit numbers but not when operands remained visible. A concurrent executive task impaired addition even with operands continuously visible, and executive-task performance worsened as carry operations increased. Thus temporary verbal maintenance and executive coordination had separable, task-dependent roles. [R02-I04, Abstract; Experiments 1–3; General Discussion]
- Trbovich and LeFevre found that working-memory interference depended on written format: a phonological load was more disruptive for horizontally presented multidigit additions, whereas a visual load was more disruptive for vertically presented additions. Both systems can contribute; display arrangement changes their relative involvement. [R02-I05, pp. 738–745, Abstract and General Discussion]

### Strategy changes the resource profile

- Imbo and Vandierendonck used choice/no-choice procedures for simple addition and subtraction. Executive load affected retrieval and procedural strategy execution, more strongly for procedures; active phonological load affected procedural strategies, and passive phonological load affected counting in subtraction. Loads did not change strategy selection in their experiments. [R02-I06, pp. 910–933, Abstract; General Discussion]
- Hubber, Gilmore, and Cragg compared instructed counting, decomposition, and retrieval in adult addition. A dynamic visuospatial n-back initially suggested strong visuospatial involvement, but a better-controlled second experiment attributed much of that cost to executive monitoring/updating. Static or dynamic visuospatial maintenance had a smaller role, while executive demand was greatest for counting. [R02-I07, pp. 936–954, Experiments 1–2; General Discussion]
- Across developmental research, the relation between working-memory measures and mathematics varies with the mathematics measure, working-memory task, age, and sample. The review abstract does not support assigning a single storage system to “mathematics” as a whole, but its full synthesis remains to be checked. [R02-I02, abstract; screened, pending full-text confirmation]

### Inner speech is variable and difficult to measure

- Alderson-Day and Fernyhough review inner speech as a heterogeneous phenomenon varying in condensation, dialogic quality, evaluative/motivational function, and sensory detail. They also emphasize methodological divergence among questionnaires, sampling, experimental interference, and neuroimaging. Inner speech should not be assumed to be continuously present or uniform across people or tasks. [R02-I01, pp. 931–965, “Phenomenology of Inner Speech,” “Methodological Issues,” and “A Multicomponent Model”]
- Evidence that articulatory suppression changes arithmetic performance supports involvement of speech-related rehearsal under that condition. It is not equivalent to a direct report that a participant heard a voice, heard complete sentences, or experienced a particular auditory quality. [R02-I01; R02-I04; R02-I06]

## Interpretations for the book

- Keep the two axes explicit: “count on by ones” is a computational strategy; silent number words, a visual tally, spatial movement, and finger tracking are possible representational formats. Experimental evidence makes those variants plausible but does not tell us their population frequencies.
- A “mental blackboard” illustration is a constructed phenomenological possibility. Presentation-format studies show that visible layout can alter resource demands, but they do not show that participants literally pictured a blackboard.
- A concise, defensible sidebar could say: verbal rehearsal can help maintain intermediate values; visuospatial storage can contribute, especially depending on layout and task; executive control is often important when steps must be coordinated. Each clause should retain “can” and specify the studied task.
- Direct retrieval should not be depicted as resource-free or universally nonverbal. Some experiments show smaller interference for retrieval than procedures, but executive involvement in retrieval is debated and task-sensitive. [R02-I06; R02-I07]
- Avoid “visual learner” and “auditory learner.” Resource recruitment changes with strategy, format, problem complexity, and whether information stays visible—even within the same person.

## Caveats

- Most dual-task findings above come from adults. They should not be translated directly into a universal sequence for elementary-school children.
- A secondary task may add general coordination, switching, or timing demands. Hubber et al.'s control condition shows why interference cannot automatically be assigned to the stimulus modality. [R02-I07]
- “Phonological loop,” “inner speech,” and “auditory imagery” are related constructs, not synonyms. Likewise, visual storage, spatial storage, visual imagery vividness, and written layout must remain distinct.
- Correlations between working-memory scores and mathematics do not identify a mechanism or prove a trainable causal bottleneck. Transfer from working-memory training to school mathematics is not established by the sources reviewed here.
- Strategy reports are retrospective and may omit fast, automatic, or inaccessible steps. Experimental task labels such as “retrieval” do not fully specify phenomenology.
- The reviewed work does not support diagnosing aphantasia, anendophasia, dyscalculia, or any learning profile from how a reader describes one arithmetic problem.

## Questions / leads

- Are there experience-sampling or interview studies that ask children about arithmetic phenomenology at the moment of solution, rather than inferring it from interference or brain activation?
- How do auditory versus written presentation and horizontal versus vertical layout change children's strategy choice and subjective experience?
- Which arithmetic operations and strategies rely more on spatial maintenance after executive dual-task costs are controlled?
- How stable is one person's reported format across problem size, operation, familiarity, fatigue, and context?
- Is there high-quality work directly comparing arithmetic in people with aphantasia or little/no inner speech? Keep this out of reader-facing claims until located and replicated.

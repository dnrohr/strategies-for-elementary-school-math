# 37 + 48 = ?

## Opening spread

The answer is **85**, but there is no single picture of getting there. A solver might separate tens from ones, jump by a round number, exchange ten ones for one ten, or recognize an intermediate fact. The first-person accounts below are **constructed representative examples**. They are not quotations from research participants.

Before reading, solve `37 + 48`. What appeared first: quantities, words, written digits, movement, or an answer without a describable image?

## Strategy gallery

## Method 01 — I split tens and ones

**Strategy class:** place-value decomposition
**Phenomenology:** visual-symbolic + verbal
**Math:** `37 + 48 = (30 + 7) + (40 + 8) = 70 + 15 = 85`

### First-person account
> A solver might describe the experience this way: “I pull the tens apart from the ones. Thirty and forty make seventy. Seven and eight make fifteen. Then I put seventy and fifteen together: eighty-five. I am adding the same amounts, just in groups that are easier to see.”

### Steps
1. Rewrite `37` as `30 + 7` and `48` as `40 + 8`.
2. Add tens: `30 + 40 = 70`.
3. Add ones: `7 + 8 = 15`.
4. Combine `70 + 15 = 85`.

### Illustration brief
Composite vector diagram with rows `30 | 7` and `40 | 8`; join tens into 70 and ones into 15, then combine to 85. Blue tens, orange ones, exact vector labels. Check the one-group is 15.

### Mathematical note
Place value permits each two-digit number to be decomposed into tens and ones; regrouping the addends preserves the total.

### Research note
Constructed account. R01-010 documents jump and compensation strategies in number tasks up to 100; it does not establish this wording as universal.

### Tags
`place-value`, `decomposition`, `visual-symbolic`, `verbal`

## Method 02 — I add forty, then eight

**Phenomenology:** spatial + verbal
**Math:** `37 + 40 + 8 = 77 + 8 = 85`

### First-person account
> A solver might describe the experience this way: “I leave thirty-seven where it is and make two jumps. The big jump of forty lands on seventy-seven. Then I take eight small steps to eighty-five. The endpoint is the answer.”

### Steps
1. Start at 37.
2. Jump forward 40 to 77.
3. Count eight unit steps from 77 to 85.
4. Record the endpoint.

### Illustration brief

### Mathematical note

### Research note

## Method 03 — I use fifty, then correct by two

**Strategy class:** compensation
**Phenomenology:** verbal + visual-symbolic
**Math:** `37 + 48 = 37 + 50 − 2 = 87 − 2 = 85`

### First-person account
> A solver might describe the experience this way: “Forty-eight is close to fifty, so I temporarily make the addend easier. Thirty-seven plus fifty is eighty-seven. But I added two too many. I take those two back and land on eighty-five.”

### Steps
1. Replace 48 with 50 and note the extra 2.
2. Compute `37 + 50 = 87`.
3. Subtract the extra 2: `87 − 2 = 85`.

### Illustration brief
Split-panel vector: `48 → 50`, then a red `−2` correction from 87 to 85. Show the equality chain and make clear 48 was a temporary benchmark.

### Mathematical note
The temporary increase and later subtraction cancel: `+50 − 2 = +48`.

### Research note
Constructed account. R01-010 (verified) reports compensation and jump strategies, with efficiency depending on problem and choice conditions; it does not recommend compensation for everyone.

## Method 05 — I turn it into forty plus forty-five

### First-person account

### Steps
1. Transfer 3 from 48 to 37.
2. Rewrite the pair as `40 + 45`.
3. Add `40 + 45 = 85`.

### Illustration brief

### Research note

## Method 06 — I line up the columns

### Steps
1. Align 37 over 48 by place value.
2. Add ones: `7 + 8 = 15`; write 5 ones and record 1 ten.
3. Add tens: `3 + 4 + 1 = 8` tens.

## Method 07 — I exchange ten ones for one ten

**Strategy class:** base-ten model / physical regrouping
**Phenomenology:** visual-concrete + motor
**Math:** `3T + 7O + 4T + 8O = 7T + 15O = 8T + 5O = 85`

### First-person account
> A solver might describe the experience this way: “I build thirty-seven with three tens and seven single cubes, and forty-eight with four tens and eight cubes. The single cubes make fifteen. I bundle ten into one new ten-rod. Now there are eight rods and five cubes. This is the physical version of the little carried 1.”

### Steps
1. Build 37 as 3 tens and 7 ones; build 48 as 4 tens and 8 ones.
2. Combine to get 7 tens and 15 ones.
3. Exchange exactly 10 ones for 1 ten rod.
4. Count 8 tens and 5 ones: 85.

### Illustration brief
Base-ten scene with exactly 7 initial rods and 15 unit cubes, then an arrow exchanging 10 cubes for 1 rod, ending at 8 rods and 5 cubes. Audit every block count.

### Mathematical note
The physical exchange makes `10 ones = 1 ten` visible; it is the same place-value fact as the written carry.

### Research note
Constructed representation. R01-007 and R01-008 (verified) support linking conceptual models and procedures, but no source here measures private block imagery during this exact problem.

`base-ten`, `regrouping`, `visual-concrete`, `motor`

## Cross-classification

The same computation can wear different representational clothes. Consider `37 + 48 = 37 + 40 + 8 = 85`:

**Add 40, then 8** (`37 + 40 + 8 = 85`)

- **Verbal:** “thirty-seven plus forty…” (Methods 02 and 12)
- **Visual-symbolic:** `37 + 40 + 8` on an imagined page (Method 11)
- **Concrete:** four tens rods, then eight ones (Method 08)
- **Spatial:** a +40 jump followed by a +8 jump (Method 10)
- **Motor:** walking or tapping the jumps (Methods 02 and 10)
- **Abstract:** the intermediate 77 appears as a stepping-stone (Method 13)

Regrouping has two exact faces. In the written algorithm, `7 + 8 = 15` writes a 5 in the ones place and carries 1 ten. In the block model, ten of fifteen unit cubes are exchanged for one tens rod. Both state `15 ones = 1 ten + 5 ones`.

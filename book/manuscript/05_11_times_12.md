# 11 × 12 = ?

## Opening spread

One product can be a remembered sound, a rectangle, a chain of jumps, or a quiet relation. The accounts below are constructed representative accounts, not quotations from research participants. We track the computation and the felt format separately.

## Method 03 — Ten twelves and one more
**Strategy class:** place-value decomposition / distributive reasoning
**Phenomenology:** symbolic + verbal
### First-person account
**Constructed solver voice — not a participant quotation**
> “I split 11 into 10 and 1. Ten twelves are 120; one twelve is 12; together they make 132. The split keeps the two partial products visible before I combine them into the final product. I can reverse the order of the addends and still get the same total.”
### Steps
1. Rewrite `11 × 12` as `(10 × 12) + (1 × 12)`.
2. Calculate `10 × 12 = 120`.
3. Calculate `1 × 12 = 12`.
4. Add `120 + 12 = 132`.
### Illustration brief
Vector bracket splits 11 into 10 and 1; labels 120 and 12; final sum aligned.
### Mathematical note
`(10+1)×12 = 10×12 + 1×12` by distributivity.
### Research note
R01-002 and R01-013 support decomposition/procedure in a changing repertoire; account is constructed.
### Tags
`decomposition`, `place-value`, `symbolic`

## Cross-classification

The same transformation—`11×12 = 10×12 + 1×12 = 132`—can appear in many formats. These are not permanent “types.”

| Format | Possible representation | Methods |
|---|---|---|
| Verbal | “ten twelves and one twelve” | 02, 03, 07 |
| Symbolic | `11→10+1`, then `120+12` | 03, 16, 19 |
| Concrete | ten groups plus one group | 03, 11 |
| Spatial | adjacent regions totaling 132 | 05, 09, 10 |
| Motor | fingers or taps track groups | 06, 17, 18 |
| Abstract | equality relation without sensory image | 01, 04, 20 |

One algorithm can have many phenomenologies, and one format can support several algorithms. A solver may switch or combine them.
